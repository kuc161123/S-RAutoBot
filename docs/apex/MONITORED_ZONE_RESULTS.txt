MONITORED ZONE TEST — FINDINGS AND RECOMMENDATION
2026-10-05

Recommendation

Keep the monitored-zone design as the next SHADOW candidate. It solves the
observed entry-expiry problem, but this test does not support activating live
trading or claiming a profitable edge. Only two trades closed; four were still
open. Every fill was long, so short performance is not demonstrated.

Test scope

Five symbols: BTCUSDT, ETHUSDT, SOLUSDT, BNBUSDT, LINKUSDT.
Primary: 2026-02-24 00:00 UTC to 2026-05-25 00:00 UTC.
Later validation: 2026-06-20 00:00 UTC to 2026-09-18 00:00 UTC.
Four predeclared arms, two 90-day windows, five symbols: all 40 runs completed.
No missing requested window, replay price-data errors or unfinished runs.
The May 25 missing hour separates the source segments; no gap was filled or
position carried across it. The later period starts from its own fixed history
origin, with 25 complete daily warmup bars, rather than borrowing pre-gap data.
Later results are chronological validation, not a guarantee of untouched data.

Rules were recorded before the experiment in MONITORED_ZONE_PROTOCOL.txt.
The numerical Elliott engine, original stops/targets and R:R thresholds stayed
fixed. The main monitored arm waits up to the engine's existing 30-day setup age.
Its 48-hour control isolates the effect of allowing longer waits. Current quote
and capped execution price must be inside the frozen zone and pass fresh risk.
No reversal-confirmation evidence is invented. Setup invalidation remains final.

What the test establishes

1. The 48-hour expiry misses daily-wave arrivals. Successful entries waited
   approximately 2.5–14.3 days. Monitoring for longer produced fills in both
   windows while the 48-hour monitored control produced none.
2. Fresh R:R matters. In the primary period, some in-zone BTC, ETH, BNB and LINK
   observations still failed risk checks. The bot kept waiting or cancelled when
   the plan became invalid; it did not move the stop or reduce the R:R hurdle.
3. Entering more trades is not proof of a better edge. Four of six positions
   remain unresolved. These results do not establish that monitored execution
   beats a structurally managed resting order with the SAME 30-day lifetime;
   that resting-duration comparison was not one of the predeclared arms.
4. Position holding time is a separate issue. One winner required 54 days and
   SOL remained open after nearly 59 days. The existing management stops timing
   out a position once it has seen a favorable 4H close. Funding and capital
   tied up in slow trades must be measured before judging practical returns.

Next recommendation

Validate this exact policy with finer execution data or a cloud shadow stream,
recording actual bid/ask, funding and rejected entries. Keep a visible watched-
setup state, fresh entry checks, fixed invalidation, price cap and risk limits.
Track aging positions explicitly, and evaluate any maximum holding/decay rule
as a separate predefined experiment, not an after-the-fact rescue of this test.
Accumulate materially more closed trades in both directions before deciding
whether automatic live entries are justified. Do not reduce R:R just for activity.

Execution assumptions and limits

Hourly close plus/minus half an assumed 0.01% spread proxies the observed quote.
The next hour's open, half-spread and 0.03% adverse entry slippage determine fill,
subject to the 0.05% cap from the observed quote. These are assumptions, not
recorded historical order books. Brief intrahour zone visits can be missed.
Full hourly bars are required to validate the replay; entry-bar targets receive
no credit and stops win ambiguity. Exits include 0.03% slippage and fills 0.06%
fees. R:R is checked at the worst permitted capped entry, not at an optimistic
historical low/high. Funding is unknown and could materially alter long holds.
No liquidity/partial-fill/outage model, AI selection, macro gate or portfolio
loss-circuit path was replayed. These are independent symbol diagnostics.
One accepted order attempt per plan was used; no failed IOC occurred here.
