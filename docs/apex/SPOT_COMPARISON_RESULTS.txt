SPOT VERSUS LONG-ONLY PERPETUAL: COMPLETED RESEARCH
2026-10-05

Verdict

Spot did not improve this version of the Elliott strategy in the tested
sample. Both arms made a small net profit, but spot returned only 0.18% and
had the larger drawdown. Do not switch live execution or increase risk on
these results. The low trade count, profit concentration and previously seen
period do not establish a dependable edge for either market type.

Fixed comparison

BTC, ETH, SOL, BNB and LINK against USDT. Each arm shares ONE 10,000 USDT
account across all five coins. Long-only; no borrowing or leverage advantage.
Cautious 0.25% risk before setup reductions; existing account/bucket caps,
daily/weekly loss gates and drawdown rules. Pending orders reserve cash and
fees. Same daily-wave/4H structure rules, 30-day zone watches, hourly next-open
IOC proxy, stops, target splitting, invalidation and structural management.

Manage holdings through 2026-05-25 00:00 UTC, another 180 days.
Common warmup: 247 days from 2023-03-25. No price gaps in either test arm.
Both full runs finished; no truncation, forced end exits or source changes.
All trades actually closed before the entry cutoff. The additional six

What drove the difference

Spot avoided funding, but its higher fee scenario and different market prices
changed eligible wave setups, entry times and subsequent management. Those
trade-path differences outweighed the financing saving. This is a comparison
of complete market-specific strategies, not identical fills with fees removed.
BNB supplied the largest per-symbol difference. Both arms lost money in BTC,
ETH and LINK, with profits concentrated in BNB and SOL. Selecting only the
winners after viewing this table would be another unvalidated hypothesis.

The system barely deployed its cash and found only 18-19 filled opportunities
over two entry years. It needs stronger evidence on entry quality and broader
opportunity coverage before more capital or leverage. A larger symbol list is
not itself proof of better results. A future validation should freeze the rules
and evaluate unseen data or forward paper trades, with actual Bybit spot
instrument rules and a point-in-time liquidity universe.

For context only, a fully invested equal-weight spot buy-and-hold basket ended
at approximately 16,014.27 USDT after modeled liquidation costs, with 58.89%
maximum marked drawdown. Its continuous exposure and lack of stops are very
different; it is not a risk-matched benchmark or a recommendation to buy it.

Data, costs and limitations

Bybit public candle access returned HTTP 403 from this environment. Both
arms therefore use the SAME alternative venue: official Binance hourly spot
and USDT perpetual archives, plus Binance historical funding rates. This is
not a replay of Bybit fills. Fee assumptions are the published Bybit base VIP0
scenario: 0.10% spot and 0.055% perpetual per execution, not verified account
fees or reconstructed historical fee tiers. Spread assumed 0.01%; entry and
exit slippage 0.03% each. No order-book depth or partial entry-fill model.

Spot buys deduct their fee in coins. The simulation sizes net owned quantity,
reserves the corresponding gross purchase cash, and never sells fee coins it
does not own. Net quantity uses a synthetic 0.0001 lot/tick convention; actual
gross purchase rounding, dust and historical exchange instrument rules are
not reconstructed. Perpetuals reserve full principal plus fees for a matched
1x cash budget. This does not validate exchange liquidation or leverage use.

Funding records retain their actual millisecond settlement timestamps. The
replay uses only rates already settled at each decision. Charges/credits use
remaining ENTRY notional, not historical settlement mark prices, so their
amounts remain estimates. They affect cash and sizing chronologically.

795 ZIP archives passed their official SHA256 checks. The normalized data
contains 27,768 consecutive hourly candles for each of ten price histories
and 3,472 funding records per symbol. A missing March 2023 spot hour and
earlier SOL perpetual gaps were excluded only from warmup using a predefined
common-origin rule. No candles were interpolated. The initial acquisition
audit also preserves a rejected older SOL funding-interval case; the retained
paired history passed its eight-hour coverage check.

Five fixed surviving assets are not a rotating historical top-50 universe.
The period overlaps earlier research and is not untouched out-of-sample data.
No historical AI/macro veto, tick execution, full exchange account behavior
or live Railway/Telegram run is claimed. These shared-account returns are
not directly comparable with earlier sums across separate 10,000-per-symbol
accounts, which also used a different venue/history origin and cost model.

Verification and reproducibility

Full Apex suite: 1,438 passed, one disposable-Postgres test skipped, three
local Python 3.9/LibreSSL dependency warnings. After coverage normalization
changes, all 14 source/coverage tests and 52 portfolio tests passed. The
accounting module has 101 tests covering base fees, cash conservation, partial
exits, missing data, reservations and model isolation.

An independent reconstruction from saved fills verified coin inventory,
fees, funding chronology, cash, final equity, W/L counts, entry cutoff and
source hashes for both arms. Full source, protocol and dataset checksums
remain unchanged through the experiment. Frozen code copies are retained.
