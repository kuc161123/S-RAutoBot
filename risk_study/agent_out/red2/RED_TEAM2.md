# RED TEAM 2 — attacking the GLOBAL rr=10/atr_mult=3.0 recommendation

Mandate: destroy the recommendation to replace the bot's 728 fitted per-symbol
`(rr, atr_mult)` configs with a single global `rr=10, atr_mult=3.0`. All numbers below were
computed fresh from `risk_study/*.parquet` and `risk_study/results/*`, or generated by new
scripts in this directory (`extend_atr_grid.py`, `attacks_235.py`) — not taken from the
target's own summaries on trust.

---

## (a) Attacks that LANDED

### A1 — atr_mult=3.0's entire holdout win is outside the range the bot has ever searched

The live bot's walk-forward has only ever chosen `atr_mult ∈ {1.0, 1.5, 2.0}`. Restricting
the challenger set to that explored range, using `risk_study/results/global_arms.csv`
(same engine, same trailing exit, same everything — just filtering the arm list):

| arm | atr_mult | final $ | holdout % |
|---|---|---|---|
| GLOBAL rr10/atr**2** (best arm with atr≤2) | 2.0 | 15,444 | **−11.7%** |
| GLOBAL rr8/atr1.5 | 1.5 | 14,274 | −39.4% |
| GLOBAL rr8/atr2 | 2.0 | 12,604 | −13.3% |
| GLOBAL rr5/atr1.5 | 1.5 | 9,032 | −42.1% |
| A0 LIVE fitted (incumbent) | mixed 1.0–2.0 | 8,587 | −44.8% |
| GLOBAL rr5/atr2 | 2.0 | 7,245 | −14.9% |
| **GLOBAL rr10/atr3 (the recommendation)** | **3.0** | **20,775** | **+6.9%** |

**Every single global arm with `atr_mult ≤ 2.0` still has a *negative* holdout return.**
The one thing that turns the holdout positive — the headline `+6.9%` — is specifically
`atr_mult=3.0`, a value one full grid-step outside anything the bot's own optimizer has ever
been allowed to pick. Global does beat fitted *relatively* even at atr≤2 (−11.7% beats
−44.8%), so "per-symbol fitting is noise" survives this attack — but "deploy rr=10/atr=3.0
specifically, in the black" does not: that number was reached by extending the search past
the boundary the rest of the system respects, and the positive-holdout claim rests entirely
on that extension.

### A2 — is atr_mult=3 a real local peak, or the edge of an unbounded "wider is better" gradient?

Extended the grid past the study's own cap (`atr_mult ∈ {2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10}`, `rr=10` fixed,
CHOP<52 gate, fixed-TP exit — same resolution rule as `build_param_grid.py`, run fresh
against the full 275-symbol universe): see `extend_atr_grid.py` /
`extended_atr_grid_raw.parquet` (247,891 resolved trades).

| atr_mult | n | stop-out % | unresolved % | mean hold (bars, stop-outs) | mean hold (bars, wins) | net R/trade |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 | 48,393 | 89.3% | 0.6% | 65.6 | 371.7 | **+0.068** |
| 3.0 | 47,588 | 89.8% | 1.5% | 142.7 | 827.1 | +0.053 |
| 4.0 | 45,660 | 90.3% | 2.8% | 226.6 | 1398.3 | +0.011 |
| 5.0 | 42,653 | 91.2% | 4.3% | 308.6 | 1740.2 | **−0.079** |
| 7.0 | 35,637 | 92.2% | 6.1% | 471.5 | 1667.3 | −0.180 |
| 10.0 | 27,960 | 92.3% | 7.1% | 777.1 | 1773.6 | −0.180 |

Two findings, cutting in opposite directions:

- **The stop-out fraction stays flat at ~89–92%** across the whole range instead of falling
  toward zero. So the "this only works because the stop never gets hit, i.e. it's secretly a
  different strategy" theory is **wrong** — win rate stays roughly constant (RR=10 is
  intrinsically ~10% WR regardless of how wide the stop is, because TP scales with the stop).
- **But net R is not monotone in atr_mult — it peaks around 2.0–3.0 and then degrades
  steadily**, going negative past atr_mult=4. So there is no unbounded "wider is free money"
  gradient; 3.0 sits close to a real local optimum on this (admittedly narrower: fixed-TP,
  no-trail, rr=10-only) test. This *undercuts* the "degenerate objective" theory. It does
  **not**, however, rescue the point estimate from A1 above — a variable that swings from
  +0.068 to −0.180 net R/trade over one order of magnitude in a single knob is a highly
  sensitive parameter chosen from a 4-point grid, precisely the kind of overfitting surface
  the whole study exists to distrust, even if this particular sample happens to land near the
  peak.

Caveat: this used the fixed-TP exit and rr=10 only (not the trailing exit or the full rr×am
grid) for tractability — a limitation, not a hidden result reversal; see failed attacks below
for a trail-exit cross-check that agrees with the direction.

### A3 — a materially larger, undisclosed alternative dominates every arm in the study's own comparison, and was never adjudicated

`risk_study/compare_bots.py` (in `risk_study/`, evidence available to this study) ran a
six-arm comparison that finished during this review (`results/compare_output.txt`). It
includes an arm the recommendation's framing never engages with: **OLD-BASE = the existing
728 fitted per-symbol configs, with the FIXED take-profit exit** (i.e. the bot's actual
deployment *before* the trailing stop went live on 2026-08-02):

| arm | final $ (continuous 3.15yr) | ROI% | maxDD% | median $ across 8 start dates | wins vs CURRENT (8 starts) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **OLD-BASE fitted + fixed TP** | **132,605** | 8,740% | 70.2% | **95,931** | **8/8** |
| CURRENT fitted + trail (incumbent) | 8,587 | 472% | 64.9% | 8,055 | — |
| **GLOBAL rr10/atr3 + trail (recommendation)** | 20,775 | 1,285% | 47.5% | 9,886 | 6/8 |
| GLOBAL-FIX rr10/atr3 + fixed | 1,334 | −11% | 75.7% | 829 | 0/8 |
| GLB-10/2 + trail | 15,444 | 930% | 54.6% | 5,768 | 2/8 |
| GLB-8/2 + trail | 12,604 | 740% | 55.2% | 5,137 | 2/8 |

OLD-BASE beats GLOBAL (the recommendation) at **every one of the 8 independent restart
dates** — median outcome ~9.7x higher ($95,931 vs $9,886) — and is the only arm to beat
CURRENT 8/8. This is the single largest effect size in the entire study, dwarfing the
GLOBAL-vs-FITTED comparison the six criteria were built around, and it points at a completely
different lever (exit rule, not entry parameters) than the one under review. Nothing in the
protocol or the six-criteria writeup mentions it, and it is not one of the pre-declared arms
A0–A5.

**This needs an immediate caveat, which I checked myself rather than taking the raw number at
face value**: OLD-BASE's dollar dominance is itself a profit-concentration artifact, and a
severe one. Direct measurement on `universe_trail.parquet`'s `r_fixed` column (32,913 trades,
CHOP<52, 24.2bps cost): **top 1% of trades = 40.4% of total net R, top 5% = 200.5%** (i.e.
the bottom 95% of trades net to roughly *minus* the top 5%'s gain). `CLAUDE.md` §6.1
documents this exact failure mode as the reason the trailing stop was deployed in the first
place ("top 1% of trades = 112% of profit" for the pre-trail fixed-TP exit over a 15-month
window) and explicitly rejects judging the fixed-TP exit on raw dollar returns for exactly
this reason. So OLD-BASE is very likely **not** a safe alternative recommendation either —
but its mere presence, undisclosed and un-adjudicated, in the study's own comparison table
means the choice to only compare arms that share the trailing exit was never argued for in
the evidence I was given; it has to be inferred by reaching back into an unrelated document.
A recommendation that silently holds "exit rule = trailing" fixed, when the study's own data
shows that dimension has 5–15x more effect on outcome than the parameter dimension being
recommended, has not actually isolated the variable it claims to have isolated.

### A4 — the study's own agents disagree, in writing, about whether criteria 3 and 5 passed

`results/criteria_output.txt` (the numbers cited by the recommendation) prints "CRITERION 3
… → PASS" and reports Criterion 5 without a verdict line. `agent_out/NULL_TEST.md` — an
independent agent given the identical task — computes the *same underlying numbers* (A0 at
the 0.0th percentile of the random-assignment null; best-of-8 beats A0 in 100% of resamples)
and explicitly verdicts:

> "Criterion 3 verdict: **FAIL**" … "Criterion 5 verdict: **FAIL**"

reasoning that A0 (the fitted incumbent) failing to beat a random parameter draw is itself
disqualifying, and that the concentration test breaks in the hostile (A1-only-trimmed)
variant at 2% removed. The "cleared all six pre-registered criteria" framing in the
recommendation elides this — two agents working from the same numbers reached opposite
PASS/FAIL labels, because the protocol's criterion 3 wording ("beats C1 by more than the CI
half-width") is genuinely ambiguous about whether it's A1-vs-random or A0-vs-random that
must clear the bar, and criterion 5's "advantage not concentrated in top 1%" is silently
being evaluated at 2% in one write-up.

Worse, the two documents' own **concentration statistics for the identical A0/A1 arms don't
match each other**: `criteria_output.txt` reports A1's top-1% share of *total* net R as
42.2%; `NULL_TEST.md` reports A1's top-1% share of *positive* net R as 8.5% — different
denominators (net-of-losers vs. winners-only) produce numbers 5x apart for a statistic
presented under the same label in both documents. Neither is wrong given its own definition,
but the recommendation cites this criterion as cleanly PASS without flagging that its
supporting evidence is internally inconsistent on the exact number that criterion turns on.

### A5 — three of four individual walk-forward fold CIs include zero; "wins 4/4" overstates the evidence

From `results/criteria_output.txt`'s own fold table plus a fresh weekly block-bootstrap CI
per fold (`attacks_235.py`, Attack 3b):

| fold | diff (A1−A0) | 95% CI | excludes zero? |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | +0.2577 | [+0.0256, +0.4962] | **yes** |
| F2 | +0.0614 | [−0.0640, +0.2007] | no |
| F3 | +0.1654 | [−0.0507, +0.4135] | no |
| F4 | +0.0321 | [−0.0839, +0.1377] | no |

Only **1 of 4** folds is individually significant. "4/4 wins" is a sign count on point
estimates, not on significance — and the folds are not independent draws (nested,
overlapping training windows sharing the same bull/bear macro regime), so even the naive
`P(4/4 | fair coin) = 6.25%` overstates how surprising a 4/4 sweep should be. The holdout
itself does clear a real bar though (block-bootstrap CI [+0.0102, +0.3956], excludes zero,
independently reconfirmed in Attack 3 below) — it's specifically the *training-fold* "4/4"
claim that is weaker than it sounds.

### A6 — GLOBAL's edge is concentrated on the same pairs the walk-forward already selected; the arms built to test this were never run

Using `grid_inuni.parquet` (fixed-TP exit, same CHOP<52 gate) split by whether
`(symbol, div_type)` is one of the 728 pairs `config.yaml` selected or one of the 376
in-universe pairs the walk-forward rejected:

| | n | GLOBAL rr10/atr3 net R/trade |
|---|---|---|
| on SELECTED pairs | 129,475 | **+0.1235** |
| on REJECTED pairs | 57,359 | **−0.1776** |
| on ALL in-universe pairs (no selection at all) | 186,834 | **+0.0311** |

GLOBAL is solidly negative on the pairs the walk-forward rejected, and its edge on the full,
unselected universe (a proxy for the protocol's own **A5 "no-selection" arm**) is roughly a
**quarter** of the headline number measured only on the selected 728. The protocol
pre-declared exactly the arms built to separate "is the parameter global-vs-fitted, or is
it the symbol/pair selection, doing the work" — **A2 (honest walk-forward per-symbol), A3
(shrinkage), A4 (liquidity-only selection), A5 (no selection)** — and none of them appear
anywhere in `results/` or `agent_out/` (confirmed by `grep -rl "A4\|A5" .` returning only the
protocol document and the grid-builder script itself). The protocol's own DEVIATIONS section
is empty, so this scope-narrowing — arguably the single most important pre-registered check
for exactly the failure mode this study exists to catch — was never logged or explained.
This is the strongest structural gap in the whole exercise: the recommendation tests "does a
global param beat a fitted param, on a symbol set that was itself picked in-sample by the
same process that also picked the fitted params" — which is not the same claim as "should
the bot trade this way," and the delta between selected-pair and no-selection performance
above (+0.1235 vs +0.0311) shows that gap is not small.

### A7 — real, quantified operational cost of the wider stop (brief's attack #6)

From the paired A0/A1 set (`attacks_235.py`, Attack 6):

- Stop distance: A0 (fitted) median `stop_frac` 1.79% vs A1 (global atr=3.0) median 4.10% —
  **~2.3x wider**. At fixed $ risk per trade this means roughly **half the notional** per
  trade for A1, i.e. proportionally smaller fills, more exposure to minimum-order-size
  rounding on cheap alts (flagged as unquantifiable further without live tick-size data), and
  materially different margin headroom versus what `config.yaml`'s current per-symbol sizing
  assumes.
- Holding time: A0 median 6.0 hours vs A1 median 33.0 hours (mean 15.4h vs 65.7h) —
  **~4–5x longer holds**. More funding-fee accrual, more weekend/macro-gap exposure per
  trade, and a position occupies its `(symbol, side)` anti-pyramid slot roughly 4-5x longer,
  which (per `REPLICATION2.md`'s own finding) is exactly the mechanism that made GLOBAL block
  24.6% of its own candidate entries to anti-pyramid vs A0's 8.8% — a real capacity cost that
  compounds with account size, not something the trade-level R comparison captures at all.

This doesn't invalidate the recommendation, but it means "swap one number in config.yaml" is
not an accurate description of the operational change — margin sizing, minimum notional
handling, and funding-cost assumptions all need re-validating before deployment, and the
higher blocking rate warrants checking whether anti-pyramid capacity effects were already
priced into the A0-vs-A1 R comparison (they are, since it's measured on realized trades) or
only into the portfolio $ simulation (they matter more there, and diverge more between
independent implementations, per REPLICATION2.md).

## (b) Attacks that FAILED

### Cost sensitivity (brief's attack #5) — the opposite of the feared result

Recomputed net R at 11/18/24.2/34.1/45 bps on the paired A0/A1 set:

| cost | A0 netR | A1 netR | A1−A0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11.0 bps | +0.1522 | +0.2297 | +0.0775 |
| 18.0 bps | +0.1044 | +0.2090 | +0.1047 |
| 24.2 bps (used) | +0.0620 | +0.1908 | +0.1287 |
| 34.1 bps | −0.0056 | +0.1615 | +0.1671 |
| 45.0 bps | −0.0800 | +0.1294 | **+0.2094** |

The GLOBAL-vs-FITTED advantage **grows** as assumed cost rises — it is not a cost-model
artifact, and if anything the 24.2bps convention is conservative toward the recommendation,
not generous to it. This attack line is refuted by the data.

### "Is atr_mult=3 secretly a strategy that never stops out" — refuted

Covered in A2 above: stop-out fraction is flat at ~89–92% across atr_mult 2 through 10. It is
not a vanishing-stop degenerate case.

### "The holdout is a lucky six-week draw" — mostly fails

Rolling 42-day windows stepped weekly across the full 2023-06→2026-07 history (159 windows,
`attack3_rolling_windows.csv`): GLOBAL beats FITTED in **116/159 = 73.0%** of them — well
above a coin flip. The pre-registered holdout's own diff (+0.2001) ranks at the **66th
percentile** of that distribution — an above-average window for the claim, but far from the
best, and not cherry-picked from the tail. A genuine weekly block-bootstrap CI on the actual
holdout is [+0.0102, +0.3956] — thin (barely excludes zero) but real.

### Independent replication (criterion 6) — holds up

`REPLICATION2.md` is a from-scratch second implementation (no imports from the reference
engine) that independently reproduces all three headline directional claims — global beats
live on final equity, cuts drawdown, and is the only arm positive on the untouched holdout —
including a max-drawdown figure within 1.1pp of the reference for the global arm. Absolute
dollar magnitudes diverge 2.5–3x, but the authoring agent traced the gap to a disclosed,
plausible portfolio-engine path-dependency (anti-pyramid/regime-multiplier interaction), not
to a disagreement about the underlying trades (which matched within a few percent). This is
a real, not rubber-stamp, replication and it survives scrutiny.

### Market controls (criterion 4) — holds up on its own terms

A1 beats BTC buy-and-hold, the long-only basket, and a BTC 200-EMA trend follower on
ROI/maxDD in the full period and is the *only* arm (of A0/A1) positive on ROI in the holdout,
where all three passive controls also lost money. The `MARKET_CONTROLS.md` author flags the
leverage/compounding asymmetry honestly and doesn't oversell it as full risk parity — a fair
treatment that survives adversarial reading.

## (c) Revised statement of what the evidence actually supports

Solidly supported:
1. The bot's current 728-way per-symbol `(rr, atr_mult)` fitting is statistically
   indistinguishable from — or worse than — a random draw from the same 4×5 grid (A0 sits at
   the 0th percentile of 2,000 random-assignment draws). This is close to airtight and
   replicated independently. **Per-symbol fitting, as currently practiced, is very likely
   capturing noise.**
2. A single global parameter choice, evaluated honestly on the same trades, does not behave
   like noise (100th percentile) and does generalize directionally across time (73% of
   rolling windows, 4/4 point-estimate folds, independent replication).

Not supported, or supported much more weakly than the "cleared all six criteria" framing
implies:
3. That **rr=10, atr_mult=3.0 specifically** is the right choice to deploy. atr_mult=3.0 is
   outside the range the bot has ever searched; restricted to the explored range (atr≤2.0),
   *every* global arm's holdout return is still negative in absolute terms — global only wins
   *relatively*, not absolutely, inside the tested space. The positive absolute holdout
   number is manufactured by extending past a boundary nobody validated.
4. That the improvement is really about parameters at all, rather than about which
   `(symbol, div_type)` pairs get traded. The advantage is ~4x larger on the walk-forward's
   own selected pairs than on the full unselected universe, and the three arms built
   specifically to disentangle this (A2/A4/A5) were never run.
5. That "wins 4/4 folds" means what it sounds like — 3 of 4 individual fold CIs include zero.

A defensible, narrower claim the evidence actually earns: *the incumbent's per-symbol fitting
is worse than useless and should be replaced by something simpler; a global parameter is a
reasonable default; but the specific point (10, 3.0) is an extrapolated, cherry-picked-after-
the-fact value whose absolute-return case rests entirely on going outside the bot's own
explored parameter space, and the study never checked whether the gain survives an honest
re-selection of the symbol universe.**

## (d) Single strongest reason the recommendation could still be wrong

**The comparison never controls for symbol/pair selection.** A0 and A1 both trade the
identical 728 `(symbol, div_type)` pairs — pairs that were themselves chosen by the very
walk-forward process this whole study exists to distrust. Section (a) attack A6 shows
GLOBAL's edge is ~4x smaller on the full, unselected in-universe pair set than on the
selected 728, and the three pre-registered arms built explicitly to separate "does global
parameter fitting transfer" from "does the walk-forward's symbol/pair selection transfer" —
A2, A4, A5 — were never computed, with no logged reason. It is entirely possible that what
this study actually measured is "a global parameter, applied to a symbol set that was
already curated by a process correlated with historical performance, beats a differently-
fitted version of the same curated set" — which says very little about what happens if the
bot is pointed at symbols nobody has cherry-picked yet. Until A5 (or at minimum A2) is run,
"replace fitted config with global config" and "the walk-forward's symbol selection is
itself contaminated the same way its parameter selection was" are observationally
indistinguishable from the evidence in hand.
